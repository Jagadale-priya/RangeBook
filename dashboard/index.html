      Rangebook

      A same-day desk for Indian stocks and for Nifty and Bank Nifty. It names a call when the tape and the rules agree, and it shows the option to buy while that call is open. You still place the order in Zerodha Kite.

      What you use it for

      • Stocks. Open a name from a watchlist and press Analyze. You get BUY, SELL, or HOLD, with the chart and the reason.
      • F&O. Nifty, Bank Nifty, and Sensex. The API watches all three every 30 seconds in the background — you do not need three tabs open. Calls, book, and drop alerts go to Windows toasts and to Notifications in the menu. Open F&O for the live chart; Settings can still refresh the tab you are looking at.
      • Option guide. While an index call is open, the Call tab names one option (call for BUY, put for SELL) with live entry, stop, and target. The contract shown is saved on the call for later review.
      • Scalper. Short-horizon tape reads for the active symbol.
      • History. When the stop or the target is hit, the call moves to Today, then to History. History keeps Nifty or Bank Nifty only: entry, stop, and target.

      How a call is made

      Prices come from Kite after you log in. Older daily bars can come from Yahoo when Kite has no history for that symbol.

      For Nifty and Bank Nifty, the first 15-minute bar is the opening range. The model estimates the chance that the first target is hit before the stop. A first close outside that range can be a call from a 45% chance. A later call in the same direction needs 58%. A bounce after a closed call needs 48%.

      The model call is shown when its own chance bar is met. Expiry, the clock, a wide opening range, volatility, and a disagreement between Nifty and Bank Nifty are listed as alerts. They do not hide the call. One open ticket is still tracked so a second fill is not stacked on top of it.

      A stock call uses a separate daily model together with RSI, MACD, moving averages, Supertrend, Ichimoku, Parabolic SAR, and candlestick patterns. Those readings support the stock call. They do not replace the opening-range rule on Nifty and Bank Nifty.

      The levels on a call

      • Nifty. Stop 25 points. First target 30. Runner 60.
      • Bank Nifty. Stop 60 points. First target 75. Runner 150.
      • Option. The entry is the live ask when the spread is within 12% of the mid. The option stop and target follow the index points on that same call. On expiry day the strike stays at the money or one step in the money.

      What is saved

      Each finished session is stored for the next train: the day’s open, high, low, close, and volume, plus that day’s chain reading when one was captured. The evening train uses a chain print only from that bar or earlier.

      Call history stores the index ticket. Live readings and the option contract shown on the call are saved on new calls from this version onward.

      The other screens

      • Replay. Move over a saved 15-minute candle. The call is the one that could have been known at that bar.
      • Contracts. The listed future and the at-the-money call and put. A reference list. The order is the option guide on the open call.
      • Sync instruments. Refreshes the Zerodha contract list used to name that option.
      • Watchlists. Up to five lists. The first list starts with the names already on the desk.
      • Settings / Notifications. Tab refresh interval is optional. Server-side index watch stays on while the API runs. Menu → Notifications lists every desk alert; click one to open that F&O symbol.
